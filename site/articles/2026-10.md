Hacker News runs two threads at the start of every month: "Who is hiring?", where companies post, and "Who wants to be hired?", where people looking for work post. In October the second thread drew 685 posts, more than in any month since it began in May 2014. The hiring thread drew 289.

That is 2.4 people looking for work for every hiring post, up from 1.2 a year ago. Only August, at 2.6, was higher. Both threads moved: posts from job seekers rose 70% in a year, and hiring posts fell 18%. Five years ago, at the pandemic-era hiring boom, the thread had 3.5 hiring posts for every person looking. The two sides have swapped places, and in the past year the gap between them doubled.

## Job seekers per hiring post are near the record August set

October's 2.4 job seekers per hiring post was the second-highest figure since May 2014. August's 2.6 is the record. Three months ago the figure was 2.0, about the same as October once you allow for ordinary swings between months. A year ago it was 1.2, and that doubling is real.

Before 2020, hiring posts always outnumbered job seekers on HN. The figure first went above 1 in April 2020, during the COVID shutdowns, and has stayed above 1 in every month since March 2023. Through 2025 it never went higher than 1.8. This year it was 1.7 in January, about 1.4 from February through April, and has been 1.7 or higher since May. Every month since June has been higher than any month before 2026.

Our verdict is plain. By the one measure that puts HN's two job threads side by side, October was the second-most crowded month for people looking for work since 2014, and the crowding came on fast.

## Hiring posts flattened while job seekers kept coming

Hiring posts have stayed between 232 and 289 since July. October's 289 was about the same as July's 274, and the smallest October thread since 2012. August's 232 was the fewest hiring posts in any month since January 2014.

Posts from job seekers grew 26% over the same three months, to 685. They have been higher than a year earlier in each of the last seven months.

Both sides moved over the year, but the job seekers moved more. Even if hiring posts had held at last October's 351, there would be 2.0 people looking for work per hiring post, against 1.2 a year ago.

To be sure, the job seekers' thread may be growing for reasons that have nothing to do with hiring. The same people may be reposting every month, or posting may simply have become a habit for more people. We don't yet link posts to the people who write them, so we can't test either. Counting only the posts still up when we fetched the threads gives the same picture: 2.5 people looking for work per hiring post, against 1.0 a year ago.

## About 21 posts were open to new grads, a third of the 2021 count

About 21 of October's posts still up when we fetched the thread said they would consider new grads. In October 2021, at the pandemic-era hiring boom, about 63 did. A year ago it was about 23.

As a share, posts open to new grads are about where they were. A language model reads each post, and by its reading, 9% of October's posts were open to new grads, against 7% a year ago and 9% in October 2021. Since January 2020 the share has stayed between 6% and 11% every month. A new grad faces the same share of willing posts as in 2021, in a thread just over a third the size.

The share did fall before 2020. In 2011 and 2012, about 20% of posts were open to new grads. Posts back then were about half as long as today's and written in a different style, so the model's reading of them is a looser comparison than the one with 2021.

## Pay in hiring posts rose, at the top of the range most clearly

Among posts that gave a yearly salary in US dollars, the typical top of the range was $220,000 in October, up 10% from $200,000 a year ago. September was also $220,000, and those two months are the highest since the series began in May 2016. These figures are not adjusted for inflation.

The typical bottom of the range was $160,000, up 7% from $150,000. That step is less certain. The figure moves in round numbers and sat at exactly $150,000 in 15 of the 22 months since January 2025.

More posts state pay at all. Thirty-one percent of October's posts gave a yearly salary, up from 22% a year ago and 4% in October 2021, at the pandemic-era hiring boom. August, September and October were the three highest months since the hiring thread began in April 2011.

## What this data can't tell us

Hacker News isn't the job market. Its threads lean toward venture-backed startups and engineering work, and one hiring post can list one role or 20, so 289 posts isn't 289 jobs. The job seekers' thread is people who chose to post on HN, and we can't yet tell a new job seeker from someone posting for the fourth month running. Nor can the counts say why more people are posting now. The pay figures cover only posts that give a yearly salary in US dollars, about a quarter of October's posts, and those aren't a random quarter.

In October 2021, at the pandemic-era hiring boom, HN had 3.5 hiring posts for every person looking for work. Now it has 2.4 people looking for every hiring post, in a hiring thread close to its smallest since 2014.
