More people posted in Hacker News' "Who wants to be hired?" thread in October than in any month since it began in May 2014. The "Who is hiring?" thread drew 233 posts, about as many as in July. Since summer, the hiring side has stood still while the line of job seekers kept growing.

That works out to 2.5 people posting in "Who wants to be hired?" for every hiring post, up from 1.0 a year ago. Most of the change came from the seekers' side: their thread grew 80% in a year while the hiring thread shrank 26%. The posts themselves barely changed. The share open to new grads is about where it was a year ago, and so is the share that say remote. Posts that list pay list no less than they did. On HN, the job hunt got more than twice as crowded in a year, and the offers stayed put.

## There are 2.5 job seekers for every hiring post, the most on record

October's ratio of 2.5 tied August's for the highest since the seeking thread began in May 2014. Three months ago it was 1.9. Both that rise and the one from 1.0 a year ago clear the noise bar, which is about 22% for months this size.

For most of the thread's history, companies outnumbered job seekers. The ratio first went above 1 in April 2023, a few months after the Big Tech layoffs, and stayed between 0.9 and 1.6 through the end of 2025. This year it climbed again. It was 1.2 in April and has been 1.5 or higher in each of the six months since.

Five years ago, in October 2021, the ratio was 0.2. That was the pandemic-era hiring boom, when companies outnumbered job seekers posting almost five to one. October's ratio is more than 11 times that. It is also well above anything the thread saw in 2023 or 2024, when the highest was 1.6.

## The seekers' thread grew faster than the hiring thread shrank

The seekers' thread drew 581 posts, up 80% from 322 a year ago. The hiring thread drew 233, down 26% from 314. Both moves clear the noise bar. Taken alone, the seekers' growth would have raised the ratio by 80%, and the hiring decline alone by 35%. The seekers' side accounts for more of the change.

The past three months make the split plainer. The hiring thread was about the same as July's 228, and it has stayed between 198 and 233 posts since then. August's 198 was the fewest since January 2013. Over the same three months the seekers' thread grew 32%. It has been larger than a year earlier in each of the last seven months.

To be sure, more hiring posts are disappearing. Nineteen percent of the hiring posts from October's first seven days had been deleted or killed by the time we fetched the thread, up from 11% a year ago. Seeking posts went the other way, 15% against 20%. Count the removed posts on both sides and the hiring thread fell 18% in a year while the seekers' thread grew 70%. That makes the ratio 2.4, against 1.2 a year ago. The gap narrows a little. It is still twice as wide.

## Posts open to new grads held their share as the thread shrank

By our classifier's estimate, 9% of October's hiring posts said they would consider new grads. A year ago the share was 7%, and in October 2021 it was 9%. Neither gap clears the noise bar. One post in this month's thread put it in a line: "All career stages welcome (including new grads)."

The share has stayed between 6% and 11% in every month since January 2020. The big decline came earlier. In 2011 and 2012, about 20% of posts were open to new grads.

What shrank is the thread around them. Nine percent of 233 posts is about 21. In October 2021, 9% of 727 posts was about 63. A new grad reading the thread today finds the same share of posts willing to consider them, in a thread a third the size.

## More posts show pay, and the pay they show hasn't fallen

Thirty-one percent of October's hiring posts stated a yearly salary, up from 22% a year ago and 4% in October 2021. July's 26% is within the noise of October's figure. Still, August, September and October were the three highest months since the hiring thread began in April 2011.

Among posts that gave pay in US dollars, the typical bottom of the range was $160,000 and the typical top was $220,000. A year ago the figures were $150,000 and $200,000. These are not adjusted for inflation. The medians come from about 60 posts each month and move in round steps, and the bottom sat at exactly $150,000 in 15 of the 22 months since January 2025. We read October as pay holding, not as pay climbing. It held in the same year the number of job seekers per hiring post more than doubled.

## What this data can't tell us

Hacker News isn't the job market. Its threads lean toward venture-backed startups and engineering work, and one hiring post can list one role or 20, so 233 posts isn't 233 jobs. The seekers' thread is people who chose to post on HN. We don't track whether the same people post month after month, so 581 posts isn't 581 new job seekers. Nor can the counts say why more people are posting now. The pay figures cover the third of posts that list pay, which aren't a random third. And a fifth of this month's hiring posts were gone before we counted, so the hiring count is a floor.

The offers in October's thread look like last year's. The line for each one is more than twice as long.
