*Editor's note, November 4, 2026: After publishing, we checked this column against outside data and widened our allowance for ordinary month-to-month swings. Three things changed. Most of the new job seekers are outside the US: counting only US job seekers against hiring posts open to US workers, the ratio went from 0.7 to 1.0, a gap within those swings. Indeed's index of US software job postings rose 23% over the same year, while HN's hiring thread had fewer posts, not more, so these threads are not a stand-in for the US job market. And the 18% drop in hiring posts and the 10% rise in the top of posted pay are both within ordinary swings. We revised the sections affected.*

Hacker News runs two threads at the start of every month: "Who is hiring?", where companies post, and "Who wants to be hired?", where people looking for work post. In October the second thread drew 685 posts, more than in any month since it began in May 2014. The hiring thread drew 289.

That is 2.4 people looking for work for every hiring post, up from 1.2 a year ago. Only August, at 2.6, was higher. Most of the change came from the job seekers' side: their posts rose 70% in a year, while 289 hiring posts against last October's 351 is within ordinary swings. Five years ago, at the pandemic-era hiring boom, the thread had 3.5 hiring posts for every person looking. On HN the two sides have swapped places, and in the past year the number of job seekers per hiring post doubled.

## Job seekers per hiring post are near the record August set

October's 2.4 job seekers per hiring post was the second-highest figure since May 2014. August's 2.6 is the record. Three months ago the figure was 2.0, about the same as October once you allow for ordinary swings between months. A year ago it was 1.2, and that doubling is real.

From the start of the job seekers' thread in May 2014 until 2020, hiring posts always outnumbered job seekers on HN. The figure first went above 1 in April 2020, during the COVID shutdowns, and has stayed above 1 in every month since March 2023. Through 2025 it never went higher than 1.8. This year it was 1.7 in January, about 1.4 from February through April, and has been about 1.7 or higher since May. Every month since June has been higher than any month before 2026.

By the one measure that puts HN's two job threads side by side, October was the second-most crowded month for people looking for work since 2014, and the crowding came on fast.

## Hiring posts flattened while job seekers kept coming

Hiring posts have stayed between 232 and 289 since July. October's 289 was about the same as July's 274, and the smallest October thread since 2012. August's 232 was the fewest hiring posts in any month since January 2014.

Posts from job seekers went from 545 to 685 over the same three months, a gap still within ordinary swings. They have been higher than a year earlier in each of the last seven months.

Over the year, the job seekers' side did the moving. Even if hiring posts had held at last October's 351, there would be 2.0 people looking for work per hiring post, against 1.2 a year ago.

To be sure, the job seekers' thread may be growing for reasons that have nothing to do with US hiring. Most of its growth came from outside the US. Among job seekers who gave a location, 32% were in the US in October, down from 47% a year earlier. Counting only US job seekers against hiring posts open to US workers, there were 1.0 per post in October, against 0.7 a year ago, a gap within ordinary swings.

## Indeed's US software postings grew this year; HN's hiring thread did not

HN's hiring thread is not the job market. Indeed's index of US software development job postings rose 23% from October 2025 to October 2026, while HN's hiring thread drew 289 posts against 351, a gap within ordinary swings. Over five years the two agree more: Indeed's index fell 56% from October 2021, at the pandemic-era hiring boom, and HN's hiring thread fell 63%.

## Posts open to new grads held their share as the hiring thread fell to a third of its 2021 size

A language model reads each post, and by its reading, 9% of October's posts were open to new grads, against 7% a year ago and 9% in October 2021. Since January 2020 the share has stayed between 6% and 11% every month. A new grad finds the same share of posts open to them as at the pandemic-era hiring boom, in a hiring thread just over a third the size.

The share did fall before 2020. In 2011 and 2012, about 20% of posts were open to new grads. Posts back then were about half as long as today's and written in a different style, so the model's reading of them is a looser comparison than the one with 2021.

## Posted pay hit a high, but the rise is within ordinary swings

Among posts that gave a yearly salary in US dollars, the typical top of the range was $220,000 in October, against $200,000 a year ago. For a median of about 60 posts, that 10% gap is within ordinary swings. September was also $220,000, and those two months are the highest of the 61 months since May 2016 with enough posts to measure, fewer than half of which fall before 2024. These figures are not adjusted for inflation.

The typical bottom of the range was $160,000, against $150,000 a year ago, also within ordinary swings. The figure moves in round numbers and sat at exactly $150,000 in 15 of the 22 months since January 2025.

A larger share of posts state pay at all. Thirty-one percent of October's posts gave a yearly salary, up from 22% a year ago, a gap just past ordinary swings, and 4% in October 2021, at the pandemic-era hiring boom. August, September and October were the three highest months since the hiring thread began in April 2011. The number of posts giving pay barely moved, though, from about 68 to about 73. So the rise in the share doesn't mean many more posts stating pay.

## What this data can't tell us

Hacker News isn't the job market. Its threads lean toward venture-backed startups and engineering work, and one hiring post can list one role or 20, so 289 posts isn't 289 jobs. The job seekers' thread is people who chose to post on HN, and we can't yet tell a new job seeker from someone posting for the fourth month running. Nor can the counts say why more people are posting now. The pay figures cover only posts that give a yearly salary in US dollars, about a quarter of October's posts still up when we fetched the thread, and those aren't a random quarter.

In October 2021, at the pandemic-era hiring boom, HN had 3.5 hiring posts for every person looking for work. Now it has 2.4 people looking for every hiring post, in the smallest October hiring thread since 2012.
