How we measure this

Every number here comes from two monthly Hacker News threads: “Ask HN: Who is hiring?”, where companies post openings, and “Ask HN: Who wants to be hired?”, where people post that they are looking. We read every hiring thread since April 2011 and every job-seeker thread since May 2014, when that thread began.

Each month is measured at the same age

Threads keep collecting posts for weeks. To compare months fairly we only count posts and replies written in the first 7 days after a thread opens, for every month, past or present. A thread from 2014 and last month's thread are both judged on their first week. A month appears here only once its first week is over, so the newest thread is left out until it is complete.

No hindsight

Each month is computed as it looked at the time. When we decide whether a company is posting for the first time, we only look at earlier months, never later ones. Rolling averages and baselines work the same way. A test checks that computing a month from the full history gives exactly the same answer as computing it from data that stops at that month. That is also why April 2011 shows every company as new: it is the first month we have.

Code for facts, a model for judgment

Anything with a clear yes or no answer is measured in code: counting posts, spotting a dollar figure, finding the word REMOTE in the header line, checking a fixed list of stack terms. A word list applies to every month, so a zero in 2015 is a real zero.

Questions that need judgment, such as seniority, industry, whether a post describes an AI product, or how upbeat a job seeker sounds, go to a classifier called Jev. Jev sees only the text of the post, never its date or anything about how the company did later. Its version is pinned and recorded with every answer, and a new version reruns the entire history so a chart never mixes versions.

Before a measure appears here, it is scored against a reference set of about 265 posts drawn from every era. Two annotators labeled each post independently and disagreements were settled by adjudication. Both annotators were AI models, not people, so the reference set checks Jev for consistency with careful readers rather than against human judgment.

Shares are expected values

When Jev answers, it gives a probability for each option rather than one guess. A post that is 70% likely remote and 30% likely hybrid adds 0.7 to remote and 0.3 to hybrid. Monthly shares are those probabilities added up and divided by the number of posts. This keeps uncertain posts from being forced into one bucket, so shares move smoothly instead of jumping when a borderline post flips.

Then and now

Comparisons put this month next to the same month 3 months, 1 year and 5 years earlier. Shares change in percentage points. Counts and salaries change in percent. A change is marked better or worse when it is good or bad news for people looking for engineering work, and left unmarked when it is neither. Hollow circles inside the charts mark the same three earlier months.

What this data can't tell you

  • One post can advertise many roles, so a count of posts is not a count of jobs.
  • Hacker News leans toward US startups, engineering roles and the Bay Area. It is a view of that world, not of the whole job market.
  • Edited posts show their text as of our snapshot. Deleted posts are missing, and deletions have become far more common: from about 2% to 9% of posts a month through 2023 to between 13% and 23% in 2026. Many are spam or posts that broke the thread's rules. We count them but don't add them back into post counts, and show the removed share below so you can judge the post-count trend for yourself.
  • Salary figures come only from posts that state one, and the share that do has changed a lot as pay transparency laws spread.
